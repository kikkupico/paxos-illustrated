Clearly visualizes clock skew across the archipelago.

v1-07 · vol1_vector_abacus

High Severity Top Candidate for Anachronism Fix
Original Prompt & Scene Concept
"A scribe at a lamp-lit desk with an enormous abacus that has one row of beads for each island, each row labeled with a small painted island emblem (goat, olive, fish); he is copying the maximum of each row from a received slate onto his own abacus."
Visual Mistakes & Inconsistencies
  • Historical Anachronism — 18th-century glass hurricane lamp: The oil lamp on the desk has a glass hurricane chimney (invented in 1780 by Aimé Argand). Classical Greece exclusively used open terracotta or bronze saucer/spout lamps without glass chimneys. This violates the project rule: "Ancient/classical-era clothing and objects only; no modern objects."
  • Historical Anachronism — Hardcover bound books: On the wooden bookshelf behind the northern scribe, several vertically stacked, leather-bound codices with ribbed spines appear alongside scrolls. Hardcover bound books are medieval/early modern; classical antiquity utilized papyrus/parchment scrolls.
Fidelity & Strengths: The vector abacus itself is a work of genius: 10 parallel wire rods, each clearly keyed on the left frame with painted pictograms of goats, olive sprigs, fish, and branding shears. The scribe's hands and the comparison slate with punched dot arrays represent vector clock maximization perfectly.
