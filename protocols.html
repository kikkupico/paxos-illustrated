The Four Rulebooks

One Chamber. Five priests. Four protocols. Change nothing but the rulebook, and watch what the Paxons gained — and paid for — at every step from the preliminary protocol to the full Parliament of Paxos.

← back to the illustrated chronicle

The mathematicians of Paxos did not invent their protocol in one stroke. They built it in four moves, each one a restriction of the last: first a protocol that was obviously correct and utterly impractical, then a practical one, then one that actually finished, and finally one that could govern an island. The trouble is that a reader meets these four rulebooks one chapter at a time and never sees them side by side. This reel fixes that. Same five priests, same decrees, same wandering, same forgetful messengers — only the rulebook changes.

The moral of the four rulebooks

Preliminary → Basic costs nothing in safety and buys memory: three lines in the back of the ledger instead of an ever-growing chronicle. It is paid for with a stronger promise — and with the slip of paper that blows away when a priest leaves.

Basic → Complete adds no new safety at all. Every consistency argument was already finished. What it adds is obligation: act promptly, elect one president, and keep balloting until something passes. Safety is a property of what you are forbidden to do; progress is a property of what you are required to do.

Complete → Parliament notices that the president never has to name a decree until step 3. So he can run steps 1–2 once, for every decree number at the same time, and thereafter pass each new decree in three message delays. The Synod chose one decree in its life; Parliament governs an island.

After Leslie Lamport, “The Part-Time Parliament,” ACM TOCS 16, 2 (May 1998), 133–169.
