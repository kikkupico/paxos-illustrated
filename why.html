How This Project Began

Why?

I recently came across Turing Award winner and the father of distributed computing, Leslie Lamport’s original Paxos paper. I was completely blown away by it! It describes one of the most important and most widely used distributed computing algorithms but does so in Greek allegory. Yes, the algorithm isn’t described with servers, databases and networks — it’s about synods, priests, ledgers and such. Eventually, Lamport had to give in and publish his algorithm with computer terms skipping the allegory. But he managed to sneak in one of the coolest puns I’ve read — he said he had to explain his algorithm in simple English because the original presentation was Greek to some.

I enjoyed the original paper but struggled to understand it not because it was written in allegory but because I wasn’t familiar with the prerequisite readings. Out of the blue, I asked myself what if all the related papers were presented in Greek allegory? This project was born!

Note that I wrote only this page’s content by myself — the original Paxos paper is used verbatim. The other papers were authored by LLMs — I think LLMs can do such literary style transfer better than I can. I remained just the audience and learner for the text and a curator for the images.
